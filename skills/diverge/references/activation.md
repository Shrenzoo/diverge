# Exploration budget

Start with the narrowest row that can settle a real choice. Openness means an implementation decision is actually delegated. A user asking for something “premium” expresses a quality preference, not permission to change their product.

| Budget | When to choose it | Work allowed | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SKIP | Exact output, prescribed implementation, active incident, or no credible improvement | Direct repair, explanation, or requested check. Zero alternatives. | Patch the login button, preserve the authentication flow. |
| LIGHT | A local choice has measurable payoff and low reversal cost | Compare the default with one small change. | An empty-state action could remove a support request. |
| STANDARD | Several mechanisms could serve an open brief | Up to three distinct directions, then implement one. | Students need to decide what assignment to work on next. |
| DEEP | An important, genuinely open problem rewards reframing | Apply an additional technique, test the strongest assumption. Stay under the candidate cap. | Rethink a study product with an explicit experimentation mandate. |

Increase breadth for a requested departure with a practical upside. Lower it for regressions, expensive verification, little time, or little freedom. A consequential decision can warrant deep **proposal** work while execution waits for approval. These are heuristics.
